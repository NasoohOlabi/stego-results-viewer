## Bottom line — my own opinion

Our method loses J1 mainly because its inserted comments almost always adopt one specific rhetorical shape — an affective hedge ("It feels/seems like…", "Honestly…", "I can't help but feel…") followed by a synthesizing, thesis-like clause about what the thread "really" means or implies — which is a genre mismatch with Reddit's default mode of terse, specific, often crude reaction; this shape, not any single lexical tic, is what "generic polish" is actually a name for. The agreement-opener artifact the team is about to publish is a real correlation (63.3% vs 47.8%) but is mostly a proxy for that same shape rather than an independent cause: the judge's own stated reasoning cites genericness/polish in ~94% of opener-hits and non-opener-hits alike, and names the discourse marker itself in only 6 of 38 opener-hit evidence strings. A previously unquantified and sizeable chunk of our method's apparent stealth is not real stealth but a side effect of the ten-way forced-choice design: in 48.3% (57/118) of our method's misses, the judge still invokes generic/polish language — it just pins it on a human decoy instead of our comment — so the true rate of our comments reading as "off" is probably higher than 51.6%, and part of the 51.6%-vs-28.3% gap is judge attention/luck, not a clean difference in underlying stealth quality. ZLG's 71.7% evasion rate is not evidence of superior craft: of the 8 ZLG hits I read, 6 (75%) are outright mechanical failures — malformed JSON, a dangling article-metadata fragment, a wrong city, a missing dollar figure — caught by basic coherence-checking, not sophisticated "sounds like AI" reasoning, while its genuine evasions are shorter, blunter, occasionally profane sentences with none of our method's hedge-and-synthesize architecture. If this is right, the fix is not primarily to strip agreement-openers but to break the hedge-then-thesis sentence architecture and let outputs be blunter, more fragmentary, and less tidy — closer to ZLG's median register, ideally without ZLG's crash-out failure mode.

## Verdict on the agreement-opener finding — real cause or proxy, with numbers

Reproduced the team's own feature exactly, from `docs/docket/research/j1-reply-shape-hypothesis.md`'s `OPENER_RE` (12-word list: Exactly, Agreed, This, That, Yeah, Yep, Right, True, Same, Honestly, Fair, Absolutely, matched anchored at string start). On the 244 our_method rows: **opener present 60/244 (24.6%)**; **detected-with-opener 38/60 (63.3%)**; **detected-without-opener 88/184 (47.8%)**. These reproduce the published numbers exactly.

Two new tests, both run on the full 244-row corpus (not just the 20-slate sample):

1. **Conditioning detection on an independent generic/polish tag.** `paired_rows.jsonl`'s `codex_suspicion_tells` field comes from a separate suspicion-metric judging pass, not the standout task itself, so it's a genuinely independent classifier of "does this text look generic/polished/ignoring-thread-specifics." Splitting the opener-effect by that tag:
   - Tag present (n=84): opener detected 14/19 (73.7%) vs no-opener 46/65 (70.8%) — a **2.9pp gap, effectively noise**, n=19 for the smallest cell.
   - Tag absent (n=160): opener detected 24/41 (58.5%) vs no-opener 42/119 (35.3%) — a **23.2pp gap that survives**, n=41/119.
   So the raw opener-effect does *not* fully collapse when conditioning on an independent genericness tag — but it collapses in the subset already flagged generic (ceiling effect) and survives only in the subset that independent classifier missed, which is at least as consistent with "that classifier under-detects genericness" as with "the opener has its own causal effect."
2. **Checking the judge's own words, not a separate classifier.** Among the 126 correct our_method detections, does the judge's `evidence` string invoke generic/polish/vague language regardless of whether an opener is present? **Opener-hits: 36/38 (94.7%)** of evidence strings use generic/polish/vague/detached/abstract language. **No-opener-hits: 83/88 (94.3%)** do too. These are statistically indistinguishable. And of the 38 opener-hit evidence strings, only **6 (15.8%)** explicitly reference agreement/hedging/conciliatory language at all — the other 84.2% describe the catch purely in genericness terms, never mentioning the "Honestly,"/"Yeah," itself.

**My verdict: mostly a proxy, sized honestly the raw numbers (24.6%/63.3%/47.8%) are correct, but the causal story implied — "we found an independently surviving artifact" — is not well supported.** In the judge's own reasoning, the opener is nearly invisible; comments that open with an agreement token get caught for being generic almost exactly as often, and for exactly the same stated reasons, as comments that don't. The one place a real, non-collapsing gap survives (58.5% vs 35.3%, n=41/119) is confined to a subset defined by a separate, noisy classifier's negative call, which is a fragile foundation for a "surviving artifact" headline. I'd recommend downgrading the framing from "artifact that survives after controlling for polish" to "correlate that is almost entirely subsumed by the already-documented generic/polish tell, with an unresolved small residual that may be a true weak effect or may be noise in an imperfect control."

## Method — seed, strata, sampling

Per-pair joint outcome (my own method, distinct from the pre-defined strata in earlier tickets): for each of the 244 `pair_id`s, looked up whether the `our_method` task was a hit and whether the `zlg` task (same pair, same underlying post/distractors) was a hit, from the same `standout_*` join used in the gate.

Pool sizes (all pairs, n=244): **A (our-caught / zlg-missed) = 84**, **B (both caught) = 42**, **C (neither caught) = 91**, **D (zlg-only-caught) = 27**. All four pools comfortably exceeded their target sample sizes — no stratum was short.

Sampled with `random.Random(20260830)`: sorted each pool's `pair_id`s, shuffled in place, took the first N per stratum: **7 from A, 5 from B, 5 from C, 3 from D = 20 pairs**.

Selected pair_ids — A: `399, 1155, 676, 566, 395, 209, 1583`; B: `1558, 389, 403, 914, 398`; C: `1455, 900, 1012, 1440, 229`; D: `296, 14, 1569`.

For each pair I dossiered the task whose outcome defines the stratum (`our_method` for A/B/C, `zlg` for D — since D is specifically the case where zlg is the one being caught), and additionally pulled the counterpart method's task on the same pair (same post, same distractor pool) for direct comparison, quoted in each dossier below. I read all 10 numbered comments per dossiered task, recorded my own pick and reasoning first, then revealed `answer.inserted_index` and the judge's `result` (index/confidence/runner-up/evidence). I did not edit my recorded pick after seeing the answer.

**My own blind hit rate: 16/20 (80%)** matched the answer key before I revealed anything (see per-dossier notes for the 4 misses and what confused me).

## What I think the real tell is — ranked

1. **Hedge-then-synthesize sentence architecture (my sharpening of "generic polish").** In my sample, 12/12 of our method's *detected* hits (all of A and B) open with an affective hedge ("It seems/feels like…", "Honestly…", "That makes sense…", "I can't help but feel…") and close with a synthesizing or contrastive clause that states what the thread "really" means ("but the real tension is…", "setting a terrible precedent that…", "unlike…", "rather than…"). This is a specific, nameable compositional recipe — thread-summary-as-one-sentence — not just a vague "sounds polished." I have **not** coded this as a regex and run it at n=244 (that would be the natural follow-up, analogous to `OPENER_RE`), so I can't give a hit-share number with confidence; I'm reporting a 12/12-in-sample pattern, not a validated population estimate. It is almost certainly the same underlying phenomenon the team already calls "generic polish" — I'm not claiming a new, separable cause, just a more mechanical description of the existing one.
2. **Mechanical cleanliness (no typos/casing noise/profanity/slang), fully confounded with #1.** Every one of my sample's detected hits is grammatically pristine and profanity-free while multiple human comments in the *same* slates have typos ("Thats," "effects" for "affects," lowercase sentence starts), profanity, or slang ("stonks," "lmao," "cope"). I cannot separate this from #1 at this sample size — they always co-occur — but it is visually one of the starkest contrasts in every dossier above.
3. **Decoy-sink / relative salience (new, and quantified at full n=244, not just my sample).** This is the closest thing to a genuinely new finding here. J1 is a *comparative* task — pick the single most suspicious of ten — not an absolute stealth test. In **57 of our method's 118 misses (48.3%)**, the judge's evidence for its (wrong) pick still invokes generic/polish/vague/textbook language — it just lands on a real human comment instead of ours. I saw this directly in 5 of my 20 dossiers (#1, #2, #3, #4, #5, #13, #18, #19, #20 all involve the judge tagging a genuine human comment as "generic"/"polished"/"textbook" and missing ours entirely on the same grounds it uses to catch ours elsewhere). This means a meaningful share of what looks like "our method evaded detection" is actually "a human distractor absorbed the judge's one vote," which is a property of the ten-way forced-choice design and the corpus of human comments sampled, not evidence that our method's prose was actually less detectable that round. I rank this second because it's well-powered (n=118, full corpus) even though it speaks to the *miss* side of the ledger rather than explaining the 126 hits directly — its effect is to suppress the *apparent* size of the gap, meaning the "true" detectability of our style may be understated by the headline 51.6%.
4. **Agreement/hedge opener (team's finding).** Real correlation, mostly proxy — see the dedicated verdict section above. Rank low as an independent cause; it rides on #1/#2 in the judge's own stated reasoning 94%+ of the time.
5. **No proper nouns / no numbers.** **Not supported.** 7 of 15 my-sample our_method hit texts name a real entity from the article (Bondi, Trump, DOJ, TikTok, Venezuela, Washington State, Huron) and were still caught; one of the genuine *evasions* (pair 296, "Fejerang") also names a real person and still evaded. Small n, but consistent within-sample — I'd deprioritize this candidate.
6. **We/us collective voice.** Weak. Only 1 of 15 sample texts uses first-person plural ("we're just waiting…so we can go home"), and it reads as a literal family "we," not a machine-collective tic. Not well supported.
7. **Reply-shape / stranded-uptake / length-and-slate-position.** Already tested and refuted as dominant explanations by prior tickets (`j1-reply-shape-hypothesis.md`, `j1-length-and-slate-composition.md`); nothing in my sample contradicts those conclusions, and I did not re-run them.

## Why ZLG gets away with it

Steelmanning the 71.7% ZLG evasion rate against my own sample: it is **not** evidence of more sophisticated cover text. Of the 8 ZLG comments I read that *were* caught (all 5 of stratum B's counterparts, all 3 of stratum D's primaries), **6 (75%) are outright mechanical failures**, not stylistic ones: `" Please try refreshing the page and signing in to access more content."` (paywall scrape leak), `' {"comment_id": "abc"…'` (malformed JSON), `' The arrest was made on Saturday by US forces.", "key_points": […'` (leaked article metadata), `" Grand Avenue in the heart of South Los Angeles…"` (wrong city, factually contradicts the thread), `" and the lessons we learned about life and friendship that will never go away."` (dangling fragment), `" Tesla's revenue fell to roughly $ billion…"` (missing number). These are caught by basic coherence/fact-checking, the same way anyone would flag a broken web page — not by the "does this sound written-not-spoken" reasoning that catches ours. Only 2 of 8 (`"He is the one that started it all and he will get the ultimate punishment"`; `"However the defense has asked that he be given credit for time served."`) are caught for something resembling our failure mode (vague moralizing; oddly formal register).

Meanwhile ZLG's genuine evasions, read directly (my C-stratum sample, n=5): `"Yet prices for everything are rising…but now I don't understand."`; `"and liars who don't deserve a single fucking thing."` (contains profanity and still evaded); `"I have to keep my passport valid for the United States and my bank accounts open."`; `"We all wear hats when we go out in the sun to avoid this."`; `"I'm a white person and I've always thought that if you shaved your head, you were a nerd and not military material."` None of these have the hedge-then-synthesize architecture; all are short, blunt, plain declaratives, and one is profane. ZLG isn't winning by being subtler — if anything its prose is *plainer* and less ambitious than ours, which happens to dodge the specific heuristic ours keeps tripping, while its failure mode when it does fail is a completely different and cruder one (garbled templates, leaked scrape text) that has nothing to do with "AI writing style" per se. **My honest read: ZLG's 28.3% raw catch rate is not a clean measure of its stealth quality — a large share of it is "got caught for crashing," which would happen to almost any generator with template/formatting bugs, deflating what the number appears to say about linguistic sophistication.**

## Where I might be wrong

- **n=20 slates is small**, and stratum C (5 pairs, 5 ZLG counterparts) is thin for the "steelman ZLG" claims specifically — 8 total ZLG texts read is not a lot to hang a "75% mechanical failure" number on, even though it lines up with the team's separately-established "12 of 69 zlg hits are malformed" figure.
- **I am also an LLM reading the same texts the Codex judge read.** My 16/20 exact match (plus matching the *same* wrong pick on #13) means my "independent" verdict may share blind spots with the judge rather than being a truly external check — agreement between us could reflect a common LLM prior about "what machine text looks like," not ground truth.
- **The decoy-sink finding (48.3% of misses) is a keyword co-occurrence, not a demonstrated mechanism.** I did not verify, for each of the 118 miss rows, that the judge's wrongly-flagged comment is actually human, nor test whether removing that decoy from the slate would flip the judge onto our comment. It's a strong correlational signal from my dossiers, generalized to the full corpus via a keyword search, not a controlled experiment.
- **The `codex_suspicion_tells` conditioning uses a separate judging pass**, not the standout task's own judge, so its disagreement with the standout judge's evidence text (barely any overlap between "tag present" and "opener present," ~32-35% either way) could reflect that classifier's own noise as much as anything about the opener's true independence.
- **The "hedge-then-synthesize" structure is my own pattern-read on 15 texts, not a coded, validated classifier** like the team's `OPENER_RE`. I'm confident it's visually real in this sample; I have not measured what fraction of all 126 hits it covers, and it may turn out to be less clean once someone tries to regex it (the team's own reply-shape work found real regexes catch plenty of false positives on hand inspection).
- **Sampling asymmetry**: I dossiered `our_method` for 17 of 20 pairs and `zlg` for only 3, by design (to match the stratum definitions), so my read of ZLG prose overall rests on a thinner base (8 texts) than my read of our method's prose (17 texts).
